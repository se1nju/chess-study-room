Italian Game, as White

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4
  • Develop quickly and aim the bishop at f7, Black’s weakest point.
  • Castle early, then play c3 and d4 to take the center.

Queen’s Gambit, as White

1.d4 d5 2.c4
  • Not a true gambit: if Black takes on c4, you can win the pawn back.
  • The goal is to pull Black’s d-pawn away and control the center.

Sicilian Defense, as Black

1.e4 c5
  • Black fights for the center from the side and gets an unbalanced game.
  • Learn one setup (Najdorf, Dragon, or Classical) well.

Caro-Kann Defense, as Black

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5
  • Solid structure and a safe king, with fewer early tricks.
  • Bring the light-squared bishop out before playing e6.

Principles that always apply

  • Control the center, develop knights before bishops, and castle within ten moves.
  • Don’t bring the queen out early or move the same piece twice without a reason.
